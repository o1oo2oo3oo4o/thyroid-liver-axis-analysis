# thyroid-liver-axis-analysis
Shared thyroid-liver genetics and hepatic triiodothyronine transcriptomics pinpoint thyroid hormone receptor beta-dependent candidate genes and a conserved xenobiotic-glutathione axis in steatotic liver disease
Thyroid hormone receptor beta agonism has established hepatic thyroid hormone signaling as an effective therapeutic strategy for metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease, yet the downstream hepatic responsive elements linking human genetic variation to direct receptor activity remain poorly defined. Here, we conducted a systematic multi-omics investigation of the thyroid-liver axis focusing on disease-relevant and hormone-responsive hepatic elements. We designed a parallel prioritization framework that integrates directional hepatic triiodothyronine transcriptomics with both locus-based thyroid-liver colocalization and gene-resolved plasma protein Mendelian randomization. Because these 2 tracks sample distinct biological compartments, their parallel integration captured both intracellular positional regulators and circulating protein effectors, defining 26 prioritized candidate genes. Interrogating this panel in thyroid hormone receptor beta-knockout human hepatocytes revealed a select set of thyroid hormone-responsive genes (GPCPD1, ADH4 and F13B) that strictly require receptor beta in human cells. We further showcased the clinical relevance of our prioritized panel across human cohorts, demonstrating that circulating candidate proteins provide non-invasive readouts of hepatic steatosis and fibroinflammation in plasma, while accounting for hepatocyte abundance in staged patient biopsies uncovers reproducible disease associations (ABHD1, F13B and ADH4) distinct from bulk fibrotic scarring. Together with the pathway-level rediscovery of a conserved xenobiotic and glutathione redox axis operating alongside classical lipid metabolism, these prioritized candidates will facilitate future investigations into less-explored mechanisms of thyroid hormone-dependent liver biology.
